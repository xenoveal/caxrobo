02 · Mechanics

How the breakout signal is built

Five stages, each a pure function of closed bars — no lookahead, no ML. This is what actually ran on every trade in this report.

Regime gate · 4H

02

Pattern scan · 1H

180 closed 1H bars scanned for triangles, flags, and head-and-shoulders via pivot geometry — no visual/ML matching.

03

Breakout trigger · 15m

Fires when the latest closed 15m bar freshly crosses the pattern's level. Volume vs. rolling average grades confidence.

04

Stop & target

Stop sits just past the broken level (buffered — see below). Target is the classical measured move.

Only ships if risk ≤ 0.5% of entry and reward ≥ 0.75% — a screen on candidates, not a sizing rule.

03 · Diagnosis

The stop was sitting inside ordinary noise

The original design placed the stop exactly on the breakout level — the level the trigger bar had, by definition, only just crossed. Median 15m bar range on BTCUSDT is 0.25%; the typical unbuffered stop distance was under 0.1%. Most "breakouts" were never wrong — they were just re-tested within one bar.

Trade outcomes: before the fix

693 trending/breakout trades · stop sits exactly on the level
90.6% of breakout trades were stopped out — most within a single 15m bar of entry, regardless of whether the breakout direction eventually proved correct. Win rate: 5.3%.

A typical loss, unpacked

Long triangle breakout · stopped out one bar after entry

Entry 55,188.3 · stop 55,074.9 (0.20% away) · the very next bar's low touched 54,928.8 — a routine wick, not a failed thesis. Price then ran to where the target was — after the stop had already closed the trade.

04 · Remediation

The fix, and where it hits a wall

Pushing the stop a small buffer beyond the level (BREAKOUT_STOP_BUFFER_PCT, still bounded by the 0.5% risk cap) gives the trade room to breathe. Swept across its full feasible range on the same BTCUSDT data:

Win rate & profit factor vs. stop buffer

buffer = 0.5% exhausts the entire risk cap → zero eligible trades
Win rate and profit factor climb steadily — confirming the diagnosis was right — but profit factor never crosses 1.0 anywhere in the feasible range, and hits a hard wall where the R:R filter starts rejecting nearly every candidate. The committed default (0.10%) was chosen conservatively rather than chasing the ceiling, since pushing further buys diminishing, still-losing returns on a single symbol's history — exactly the kind of parameter chase the project's own anti-overfitting protocol exists to prevent.

06 · Validation

The walk-forward verdict

17 rolling train/test folds, then one untouched out-of-sample holdout the tuning process never saw. This is the MVP gate from the PRD.

Fold-by-fold test expectancy · after the fix

bars below zero = losing fold · 15 of 17 folds are negative
Plateau ratios on the rare positive folds are deeply negative (as low as −11.3), meaning the handful of "winning" parameter combinations are fragile spikes surrounded by losing neighbors on the grid — not a robust plateau. That's the exact overfitting signature this harness exists to catch.
07 · Recommendation

Where this leaves the project

The stop-buffer fix is real and worth keeping — it roughly doubled win rate and cut drawdown by more than half on the trending bucket, and it's now committed with passing tests. But the walk-forward gate still fails, and the evidence (flat profit factor across the entire feasible buffer range, 15 of 17 losing folds, fragile plateau ratios) points to a genuine absence of edge in the breakout method as currently specified — not a remaining implementation bug.

Two patterns (triangle, flag) account for essentially all trade volume and both lose money; the two head-and-shoulders variants are too rare (4 trades each) to judge. Chasing further parameter combinations against this single symbol's history from here on would itself violate the PRD's anti-overfitting protocol — walk-forward re-tuning against results you've already seen is how overfit backtests get made.

This is now a strategy-design question, not a bug hunt: wider R:R bands, different entry confirmation, longer holding windows, or a redesigned measured-move target are the plausible next levers — each needs its own walk-forward validation, ideally with fresh data rather than another pass over 2023–2026. Phase 6 (confidence scoring) and beyond should stay paused until a signal method actually clears this gate.
